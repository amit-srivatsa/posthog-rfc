## Summary

PostHog ships new products faster than sales can learn to pitch them. You want someone who turns PMM work and Gong calls into material reps actually open, every week.

I propose you hire me. I've already shipped the first piece: [a battlecard for AI Observability](./battlecard-ai-observability.md). It took one afternoon, and every claim links to a source.

## Problem

From your job post, paraphrased honestly:

- Sales needs positioning, battlecards, objection handling and customer stories. Fast.
- Some features have no dedicated PMM, so nobody owns their story.
- The insight lives in Gong recordings and Slack threads. It doesn't reach the reps who need it.
- Whitepapers and quarterly trainings don't fix this. Weekly shipping does.

## Proposal

Hire a writer who builds. Specifically, one who:

1. **Writes for technical people** and keeps it short.
2. **Builds the tools** instead of filing tickets for them.
3. **Has sat with sales** and made things they used without being asked twice.

## Evidence (prior art)

### I build AI tools for sales teams

At **Solid Optics** I built two Copilot agents for the sales team: a post writer and a brand-voice reviewer, both grounded in a voice guide I wrote. Then I trained the team to run them without me.

**Impact:** idea-to-publish time for sales posts dropped from **4 to 5 days to 4 to 5 hours.** Content production time across the team fell by about **50%.**

### I write objection handling into the pitch, not after it

[The AI Edge](https://magicwand.site/the-ai-edge) is a workshop landing page I built from scratch. The objection handling lives in the page itself, next to tiered pricing, testimonials and a benefit-first structure. It's a sales conversation written down, which is what a good battlecard is too.

### I write for technical audiences

- **NetApp (2019 to 2022):** wrote for storage admins and engineers. Rebuilt the knowledge base: **20% lower bounce, 10% higher CTR.** Improved AI chatbots: **30% more engagement.** Led a CMS migration.
- **Adobe (2022 to 2023):** owned in-app education for Illustrator and Photoshop features, working with product and engineering. **+11% user acquisition, +6% trial-to-paid.**

### Vibecoded side projects (you said they count)

- [**Ask-my-career agent**](https://www.amitsrivatsa.com/agent): a live Claude-powered agent on my site. Streaming, prompt caching, guardrails in prompt *and* code, adversarially tested.
- [**Buildtober gallery**](https://www.amitsrivatsa.com/builds): small tools shipped in public, each with a demo and one lesson. Including a [voice linter](https://www.amitsrivatsa.com/builds/voice-linter) and a [LinkedIn audience map](https://github.com/amit-srivatsa/linkedin-dashboard).
- My own job search runs on a Claude Code pipeline I built: it scans job boards, scores roles, tailors the CV and checks every claim against a story bank. This repo came out of it, which is either very meta or very efficient.

I'm not a software engineer. I ship with AI coding tools daily anyway.

## Risks and counter-arguments

Every good RFC argues against itself. So:

| Risk | Honest answer | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| No devtool company on my CV | True. My technical audiences were storage admins and creative pros, not developers. | I *am* the developer audience now: I build with Claude Code, Vercel and GitHub every week. And the [battlecard](./battlecard-ai-observability.md) shows I can learn a devtool fast. |
| No Gong or formal battlecard history | True. My sales enablement was agents, training and pitch pages. | The battlecard ends with a "still missing" list of what I'd pull from Gong on day one. I know what I don't know. |
| "Developer marketing" years are thin | Strictly, zero in that title. About 3 years writing for technical audiences. | Judge the work, not the title. That's sort of your whole hiring process anyway. |

## Alternatives considered

1. **Hire someone with "Developer Marketer" already in their title.** Valid. Fewer of them have shipped an AI tool for sales *and* a battlecard for you before the first call.
2. **Let each PMM write their own sales material.** Your job post says that's the gap.
3. **Do nothing.** Reps keep asking in Slack. Nothing gets shipped in a meeting.
